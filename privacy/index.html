Privacy Policy

Morningstar: Night of Glass for iPhone · Effective October 9, 2026

The short version:

  • Your progress stays on your iPhone. The game sends nothing about you to me.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob, only between runs. To show them, Google’s ad software collects some data from your device, such as your IP address and, only if you allow tracking, your advertising identifier.
  • You choose: Apple’s tracking prompt, and Privacy choices in the game’s Settings (under About) where the law in your region requires it.
  • Ads are limited to G-rated content. There is no account, no sign-in and nothing to buy.

Who this policy covers

Morningstar: Night of Glass (“Morningstar”, “the app”) is an independent game made by Edward Carreno (“I”, “me”). This policy covers the Morningstar app for iPhone, version 1.0.0 and later, and this website.

What is collected, and by whom

By me: nothing from the game. Morningstar has no sign-up and no analytics of its own. The game never sends your progress, your scores or anything else about you to me, and it never asks for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or location. From Google I receive only combined ad reports, such as how many ads were shown in each country, which cannot identify you. If you email me, I receive your email address and what you write, and I use them only to answer you.

By Google, to show ads. The app includes Google’s advertising software, the Google Mobile Ads SDK (Google AdMob), and Google’s User Messaging Platform, which asks for your ad consent. When your iPhone is online and the app loads or shows an ad, they may collect:

  • your IP address, which Google uses to estimate your approximate location (such as your country or city);
  • device and advertising identifiers: Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking (see Tracking and privacy choices); otherwise identifiers that are limited to this app or its developer;
  • ad and app interactions, such as which ads were shown, taps on ads, videos watched and app launches;
  • diagnostics, such as crash logs and performance data (for example, how long the app takes to launch and whether it hangs).

Google uses this data to show, measure and improve ads, to limit how often you see the same ad, for analytics, and to detect and prevent fraud and abuse. Google is an independent third party and decides how it uses this data under its own policies, not mine: read How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy. Google may share some of this data with companies it works with to show and measure ads, such as advertisers and other ad networks that bid to show an ad through Google’s platform (Google’s ad technology partners). An ad shown in the app may also load its pictures, video or measurement code from the advertiser’s servers or those of companies that check and measure ads, which then see your IP address. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form lists these partners and asks for your consent first. Google handles the data it collects under those policies and the data protection terms that apply between Google and AdMob publishers.

I do not sell your personal information for money. Under some US state privacy laws, letting an advertising partner use device data for personalized ads can count as “selling” or “sharing” it. You can opt out at any time with Privacy choices (see Tracking and privacy choices).

Ads in the app

Ads only appear between runs, never while you play:

  • An optional video on the card after a run. The WATCH AD button shows the bonus shards it gives; watch the short video to the end and the shards are yours. Once per run, up to 6 times a day.
  • A full-screen ad after some finished runs, behind a short AD BREAK screen: at most every 2nd run and at least 3 minutes apart, never during play and never in your first 5 runs.
  • A small banner at the bottom of the title screen only. It hides as soon as a run starts or a menu opens.

Ads are limited to content rated G (suitable for general audiences). Many ads carry Google’s AdChoices icon, which shows why you were shown that ad. Ads need an internet connection: offline they simply don’t load, and the game plays the same.

Optional videos. Watching one is always your choice: nothing plays unless you tap WATCH AD, and the shards are given only when the video finishes. They are added to the save on your iPhone; nothing about them is sent to me.

Tracking and privacy choices

Apple’s tracking prompt. The first time you open the app, after the title appears, Google’s consent message is shown, followed by Apple’s App Tracking Transparency prompt asking whether Morningstar may track your activity across other companies’ apps and websites. If you allow it, Google may use your advertising identifier to show you more personalized ads. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the identifier is not shared and you still see ads, just less personalized ones. The game plays exactly the same either way: nothing in it is given or taken away by your answer. If Allow Apps to Request to Track is switched off on your iPhone, the prompt does not appear and the app does not track you.

To change it later, open your iPhone’s Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking and switch Morningstar on or off. Switching off Allow Apps to Request to Track refuses tracking for every app at once.

Privacy choices in the game. Where the law in your region requires it, the game’s Settings → About has a Privacy choices button:

  • In the European Economic Area (EEA), the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form asks for your consent on first launch, before your data is used for ads. Privacy choices lets you review or withdraw that consent at any time. If you do not consent, you may see fewer ads, and only non-personalized ones.
  • In US states with consumer privacy laws, Privacy choices is where you can opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of your personal information for targeted advertising.

Google’s consent tool keeps your choices on your iPhone, so it does not ask again on every launch. The Google Privacy Policy explains the other choices Google gives you.

What stays on your iPhone

To remember your progress, the app saves a small game file in its own storage on your device: your best scores and times, ranks, missions, shards, unlocked items, daily streak and settings (such as sound, music and haptics), plus a few ad counters (how many full-screen ads and optional videos were shown today, and when the last full-screen ad played) so that ads stay spaced out and the daily limits work. This file never leaves your iPhone through the app, and I cannot see it. Your iPhone’s own backups (such as iCloud Backup, if you use it) may include it, under Apple’s control.

  • Erase your progress: in the game, open Settings → Save data → Reset progress, then tap again to confirm. Your settings and the ad counters are kept.
  • Erase everything: delete the app from your iPhone. Its saved file, and the consent choices stored for it, are deleted with it.
  • Save codes: Settings → Save data can show your progress as a text code (starting with MS1-) so you can move it to another device. The code is created on your iPhone and goes only where you paste it.
  • Copying results: if you copy a Daily result or a save code, the text is placed on your device’s clipboard at your request. The app does not send it anywhere.

Accounts and purchases

There are no accounts, logins or in-app purchases. Shards, the game’s only currency, are earned by playing and cannot be bought. The optional video after a run can add bonus shards, but everything in the game can be earned just by playing.

Internet and permissions

Morningstar plays fully offline: the game, its graphics and its music are all inside the app, including in Airplane Mode. When your iPhone is online, the Google Mobile Ads SDK and Google’s consent tool connect to Google to load and measure ads and to ask for your consent; the game itself sends nothing to me. The links under Settings → About (Privacy Policy, Support and Report an ad) open this website in Safari, outside the app.

The app does not ask for access to your location (Location Services), camera, microphone, photos or contacts. Google may still estimate your approximate location from your IP address, as described above. The only permission it asks for is Apple’s tracking prompt described above. It plays sound and uses the iPhone’s haptic engine for vibration feedback, both locally.

Children

Morningstar is a general-audience game, rated 4+ on the App Store. It is not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age where you live), and ads in the app are limited to content rated G. I do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and the game has no chat, no sign-up and no way to contact other players.

If you are a parent or guardian and have a question about your child’s data, or think your child has sent me personal information (for example by email), write to the address below. I will answer, and delete anything your child sent me.

Report an ad

If you see an ad in Morningstar that seems inappropriate, offensive or not right for a general audience, please tell me: in the app, open Settings → About and tap Report an ad, or email me. Include when you saw the ad, what it showed and, if you can, a screenshot. I block reported ads in my Google AdMob account so they are not shown again. See how to report an ad.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, for example in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland or certain US states, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or receive a copy of your personal data, to object to or restrict how it is used, to withdraw your consent at any time, and to opt out of targeted advertising or of the “sale” or “sharing” of your data. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, ads that use your data rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Privacy choices.

I hold no personal data about you from the game, so there is nothing of mine to access, correct, export or delete. Data collected for ads is held by Google; the Google Privacy Policy explains how to use your rights with Google. You can always write to me and I will help. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, such as the one in your country in the EEA, the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) in the UK or the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) in Switzerland.

Keeping and deleting data

I keep no ad data. Google keeps the data its ad software collects for the periods described in the Google Privacy Policy (see how Google retains data). Your game save stays on your iPhone until you delete the app (Reset progress erases your progress but keeps your settings and the ad counters). If you email me, I keep the conversation only as long as I need it to help you, and delete it sooner if you ask.

Apple and the App Store

You download Morningstar from Apple’s App Store, which is run by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy. Apple may give developers aggregated, anonymous App Store statistics (for example, the number of downloads) and, only if you have chosen to share them in your iPhone’s settings, crash reports. These do not identify you to me, and I use them only to understand how the app is doing and to fix bugs.

This website

This site is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, analytics, ads, trackers, fonts or scripts from other services. Like any web host, GitHub may keep server logs (such as IP addresses) for security and reliability, as described in GitHub’s privacy statement. If you email me, I use your message and address only to reply to you.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Morningstar changes anything described here, for example a new kind of ad, I will update this page and its effective date, and the App Store’s privacy details, before that version is released.
